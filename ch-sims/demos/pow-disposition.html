Where did 8.5 million prisoners actually go?

Capture was rarely the end of a soldier's war. It forked into labor, parole, and a mortality rate that moved ~5× depending only on who held you — before a repatriation clock that ran, in places, three years past the armistice.

Figure 2 · Repatriation drain

Armistice ≠ freedom

The reservoir of prisoners still held drained slowly, gated by legal levers written into the peace treaties, not by the guns falling silent.

≈ 1.2M still held (Dec 1918)
Reservoir: ~1.2M Russians still on German soil (Dec 1918) → 182,748 (Oct 1919) → trickle through 1920 → last out from Siberia/Japan, 1922. Legal gates: Versailles Art. 214 (repatriate "with the greatest rapidity") and Art. 221 (a leverage clause tying release to other terms). French-held Germans held to spring 1920.

Adjustable assumptions — what moves these numbers
Total captured (±2M caveat): ~8.5M is a commonly cited aggregate; Russian-front intake is the least reliably documented figure in the whole system, so totals swing by up to ±2M across sources depending which fronts and which "captured vs missing" definition is used.
Overall mortality (~10%, ~750k): By 1916, ~90% of German-held POWs were already working — labor was the default, not the exception, which is why the labor-vs-parole slider above matters more than any single "captor" label.
Legal floor: Hague 1899/1907 Arts. 4–20 set the baseline (humane treatment, no reprisals) but had no enforcement teeth; Brest-Litovsk Arts. VIII/XII briefly restructured Eastern-front exchange before the treaty itself collapsed. The 1929 Geneva Convention (16→97 articles, reprisals banned) is the direct reform this war forced — it did not exist yet for anyone modeled here.

Caveats

Totals vary by up to ±2M across sources; Russian-front figures are the least reliable in the dataset. "Armistice ≠ freedom" — legal release lagged the guns by up to three years for some cohorts.
