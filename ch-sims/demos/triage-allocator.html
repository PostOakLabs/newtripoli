Documented record & further reading
Documented record Rubin JB et al., "Equitable Allocation and Ventilator Triage" — simulation cohort study, PLOS ONE (2024): 1 ventilator per 2 patients base case; age-banding ≈28.7 lives + 3,408 life-years saved per 1,000 (maximizes life-years); SOFA-bands ≈13.2 lives + 416 life-years saved per 1,000 (fewest lives, most equitable). All protocols except lottery and banded-SOFA produced statistically significant racial disparities in the study cohort.
White DB, Lo B et al., COVID-era crisis-standard-of-care allocation simulation, Am J Respir Crit Care Med. Caveats This page's SOFA-bands and age-band headline figures anchor on the PLOS ONE point estimates; lottery and SOFA+comorbidity outcomes, and every band shown, come from this page's own small synthetic 1,000-patient simulation (12 reseeds) built to the same tiers — not a reproduction of the study's full patient-arrival model. Age carries uncertain legal status as an allocation criterion in several US jurisdictions. Read every number here as illustrative, not a clinical recommendation. Genre / further reading (living-genre color, not scholarship, where noted) NY State Task Force on Life and the Law, Ventilator Allocation Guidelines (2015, rev.) — source of the ≤7 / 8–11 / ≥12 SOFA tiers used here.
Maryland Department of Health, Crisis Standards of Care framework (2021) — source of the +3-point severe-comorbidity SOFA adjustment.
