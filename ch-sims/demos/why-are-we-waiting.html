Guided walkthrough · the argument

Why are we waiting?

The Intake was monstrous because it was involuntary — not because it was impossible. The unsettling claim underneath the whole series is that nothing the ETI did sits off humanity's own tech tree. Walk the six steps; run the numbers yourself at each one.

1
Premise
The Intake used no alien magic.

Femtoscale manufacturing, a solar-powered substrate, brains kept alive and lit — every ingredient is something a sufficiently advanced human government or corporation could in principle build. The ETI supplied the will and removed the consent. The capability is ours to reach.

2
Energy · feasibility
Power is not the obstacle.

Eight billion preserved brains, plus their life-support, draw on the order of 0.4 TW. The Sahara — already the chosen site — can deliver roughly 230 TW. That's under a fifth of one percent, with a ~550× margin.

3
The catch · biology
Biology is the obstacle.

A brain runs at ~20 W and you cannot simply overclock tissue — neural firing, synaptic delay, the binding rhythm of consciousness and neurotransmitter depletion all cap it. A pure biological vat tops out near 2×. The hard part was never the power bill; it's the meat.

4
The ladder · augmentation
But the ladder has rungs.

Replace chemical synapses with photonic ones, then more of the cortex, and the ceiling climbs — ~50×, then thousands, then substrate speed. Each rung trades away a little more biology, and the question stops being "can we power it?" and becomes "is it still the same mind?"

5
The payoff · time
Then time becomes abundant.

Once the substrate can run fast, a single calendar year buys subjective centuries — and at the series' upper rates, a million subjective years per year. Lifetimes of art, study, and company, folded into ordinary wall-clock time. That is the prize the dilation tool measures.

6
The question

So why are we waiting?

San Junipero is on the tech tree. The energy is lying in the desert; the biology is an engineering problem with visible rungs; the payoff is centuries of subjective life. The only thing the ETI added was the decision to build it — and the refusal to ask first. The series leaves the reader holding the obvious follow-up.
