Synthetic-Body Spec Sheet

At the Wake (§35), everyone returns in a durable synthetic body. The canon build uses an osmium skeleton — extraordinary toughness at a brutal price. Toggle the skeleton material and watch what your body can and can't do.

◆ In-world canon · §35 / §37

The skeleton is most of the body's mass, so the material you pick cascades into everything else — what you weigh, whether you float, what you can ride, how hard you are to break, and what it takes to fix you. The ETI chose osmium for sheer durability; the cost is a body that behaves like a small engine block. Switch materials below and read the consequences.

FIG // Why the skeleton material decides everything
Everything to the right of the float line sinks. For scale, bone is about 1.8 g/cm³; an osmium skeleton is 22.6 — over twenty times denser than water — which is why the canon body is so heavy and so completely unsinkable. Lighter skeletons creep back toward the float line, trading away durability for the ability to swim and ride.

The canon body (§35) uses an osmium skeleton — osmium is the densest stable element at 22.59 g/cm³, about 3× iron and 5× titanium — giving a total mass around 450 lb (204 kg), roughly three adult humans. Mass here scales with skeleton density (the skeleton dominates), anchored to the canon osmium figure; it's illustrative, not a structural FEA.

Consequences are canon: minimum viable personal vehicle is a heavy-duty motorcycle; entering water means "immediate forceful sinking"; recharge runs an 8-hour cycle for ~2 weeks of continuous motion, with a "go to sleep" safety mode. Crucially, the brain and deep osmium structure are irreparable in the field — the precision fabrication needed doesn't exist until the bootstrap tech-tree reaches it (§36). Lighter skeletons trade away durability for mobility and buoyancy.
